The read

This is the sharpest decline in the series, and its most useful feature is how even it was. Views down 33%, visitors down 32%, search down 29%, Facebook down roughly 30% per day, LinkedIn down 57%, and all four established top pages down between 16% and 48%. Nothing broke. Everything thinned. That pattern almost always means fewer inbound pointers — fewer shares, sends, or links — rather than a content or ranking problem.

Part of this is the October 5 comparison. That day drew 95 views from 80 visitors, the biggest single-day audience in any report, and last week's report flagged it as worth identifying. It was never identified, and the equivalent Monday this period drew 47. Half the week-over-week gap on that one day alone. An unexplained spike you cannot repeat becomes a decline you cannot explain.

Views per visitor held at 1.19. Third consecutive report at roughly 1.2, after three straight at 1.67. The reading depth is stable at the lower level, which confirms it as the site's genuine baseline rather than a dip. Traffic volume moved this period; depth did not. Those are two separate problems and only one of them is new.

Two compliance articles climbed while everything else fell. The debriefing piece rose 250% to 7 views and the conflict-of-interest overhaul rose 600% to 7. They are the only green numbers on the page. Six reports in, every single one has seen new compliance and enforcement pieces break into the top ranks — OCI, fraud settlements, CPARS, revolving-door rules, and now debriefings and COI again. This is the most consistently confirmed finding in the entire series, and it held even in the worst week.

LinkedIn broke below its floor. After three consecutive reports at exactly 7 views, it fell to 3. That is four reports of decline from 22 with no recovery at any point. Whatever was being posted there has stopped working or stopped happening, and it will not self-correct.

Saturday was again the deepest day. 23 views from 13 visitors — 1.77 views per visitor, the highest of the period and the second week running that Saturday produced the lowest traffic and the best engagement. A small group reads properly on weekends. It is too small to build on, but it is the only place the 1.2 ceiling breaks.

Tuesday the 13th was another perfect one-page day: 28 views from 28 visitors, exactly 1.00. That is the third such day across the last three reports. Each one is a day on which the internal links did no work whatsoever.

Four in five readers are on a desktop. 80.9%, up from 77% in early September. Mobile is 17.4%. Together with Bing overtaking Google last period, the audience profile is now unambiguous: people at a desk, on a work machine, during business hours. Design and test for that reader first.

773 subscribers. Sixth consecutive report, unchanged. The site has grown from 104 posts to 221 across this series — 117 more posts — and the subscriber count has not moved once. Traffic can rise and fall; this number has done neither. Six reports is past the point where it can be treated as a pending task.

What to do next

Ordered by what unblocks the most.

  1. Pause publishing for one week and fix the subscriber capture117 posts added across six reports, zero subscribers added. Another 24 posts this week did not change it. Open a live post on a desktop — where four in five of your readers are — and confirm a subscribe form renders, submits, and lands a test address in GoHighLevel. Until that works, every post is traffic you cannot keep.
  2. Account for the October 5 spike before writing off the decline95 views from 80 visitors on one Monday, never explained, never repeated. Check the newsletter send log and the Facebook posting history for that date. A third of this week's apparent drop is the absence of whatever that was.
  3. Feed the compliance cluster — it is the only thing growingThe debriefing piece and the COI overhaul were the only rising pages in the worst week of the series. Build the enforcement and compliance hub page, cross-link all of these pieces into it, and link that hub from the top of the NASA article, which still pulls more search traffic than any other post.
  4. Stop counting on LinkedIn22 to 7 to 7 to 7 to 3 across four reports. It has declined every period and recovered none. Either assign someone a fixed posting cadence and measure it for two weeks, or formally drop it and redirect that time to Facebook, which still delivers thirteen times the traffic.
  5. Design for the desktop reader80.9% desktop and rising. Review the article template at desktop width — line length, where the CTA sits in a 1400px window, whether the subscribe prompt is visible without scrolling. The mobile-first default is the wrong instinct for this audience.
  6. Connect Search Console and GA4Search fell 29% and this view does not even break it down by engine. You cannot tell whether that is a ranking change, a seasonal dip, or an attribution gap. Sixth report carrying this item.
